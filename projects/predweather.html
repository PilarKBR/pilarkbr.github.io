Research Overview

PredWeatherMarkov is an award-winning academic research project that earned 3rd Place in the IEOM Undergraduate Student Paper Competition, sponsored by Siemens, at the prestigious 6th Asia Pacific International Conference on Industrial Engineering and Operations Management in Bali, Indonesia (August 5-7, 2025).

This study presents the development and implementation of a Markov Chain-based model to analyze and forecast daily rainfall patterns in Bogor City, Indonesia, a region known for its high precipitation. Using daily rainfall data from July 22, 2024, to July 21, 2025, the rainfall intensities were classified into five categories according to BMKG standards: No Rain, Light Rain, Moderate Rain, Heavy Rain, and Very Heavy Rain.

A transition probability matrix was constructed by calculating the relative frequencies of state changes, and multi-step predictions were generated using the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation. The model reached a steady state after 16 iterations, with results showing an accuracy of 60%, a recall of 100%, and a precision of 55.56%. These findings indicate that the model performs well in identifying rainfall events, especially light rain, but also produces a notable number of false positives.

Technical Architecture

Markov Chain Implementation

The core prediction engine utilizes first-order Markov chains to model weather state transitions. In this probabilistic approach, the future state depends only on the present state and not on the sequence of past states:

Rainfall Classification (BMKG Standards):

  • State 1 - No Rain (NR): 0 mm/day
  • State 2 - Light Rain (LR): 0.1 - 19.9 mm/day
  • State 3 - Moderate Rain (MR): 20.0 - 49.9 mm/day
  • State 4 - Heavy Rain (HR): 50.0 - 100.0 mm/day
  • State 5 - Very Heavy Rain (VHR): >100.0 mm/day

Research Methodology & Results

The research employed rigorous statistical analysis and experimental validation following a systematic approach:

Methodology

  • Data Collection: Secondary data from BMKG platform covering 365 consecutive days (July 22, 2024 - July 21, 2025)
  • Categorization: Rainfall intensities classified into five groups using BMKG official classification
  • Statistical Methods:
    • Maximum likelihood estimation for transition probabilities
    • Chi-Square test for independence evaluation of consecutive rainfall occurrences
    • Chapman-Kolmogorov equation for multi-step predictions
  • Model Validation: Comparison of predicted vs. actual rainfall data for July 12-21, 2025

Performance Metrics

  • Overall Accuracy: 60% (6 out of 10 predictions matched actual conditions)
  • Precision (Light Rain): 55.56% - Indicates the model produces a considerable number of false positives
  • Recall (Light Rain): 100% - All light rain events were successfully identified with no false negatives
  • F1 Score: 71.43% - Reflects the balance between precision and sensitivity
  • Steady State: Achieved after 16 iteration steps, demonstrating long-term stability

Key Findings

  • High Recall Performance: The model is very effective at detecting light rain, minimizing the risk of missed rain events
  • Early Warning Capability: 100% recall makes it suitable for early warning or logistical preparation applications
  • Tendency to Overpredict: Lower precision (55.56%) indicates the model tends to "play it safe" by frequently predicting light rain
  • Seasonal Patterns: Data reveals distinct patterns - rainy season (October-March) and dry season (April-September)
  • Practical Limitation: Beyond 16 steps, predictions converge to steady state, requiring improvements for longer-term forecasting
